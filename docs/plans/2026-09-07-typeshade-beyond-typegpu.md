## 0. Executive summary

**To surpass TypeGPU.** "Surpass" has one arithmetic meaning here: a CA §2.2 composite above **4.80**, i.e. an index above **55.2** (`index = composite ÷ 8.70 × 100`, derived). TypeShade is at **3.35 / 38.5**, so the gap to TypeGPU is **+1.45 composite / +16.7 index**, and the gap to TypeShade's own P90 is **+1.70 / +19.5** [re-run]. Every named move in CA §3 except one is worth +0.10 or +0.20; only WGSL ingestion of an admitted subset is worth +0.60. The consequence is arithmetic, not judgement: **CA's P50 vector lands at 4.05 / 46.6, and this document's step-5 bundle — P50 plus one P90 element, the repriced pitch — lands at 4.15 / 47.7, still 7.5 index points BELOW TypeGPU** [re-run]. Adding ingestion reaches 4.75 / 54.6 — **still below**. Crossing needs one further +0.10 (a third host, C7 4→5) for 4.85 / 55.7, a margin of half an index point that survives **neither** load-bearing cell moving down one notch (§2.1).

**Abandoning ingestion does not cap the programme at 4.15.** Steps 7 (a third host) and 8 (a second publisher) are independent of ingestion and survive its kill criterion, so the honest no-ingestion ceiling is **4.45 / 51.1** — 4.1 index points below TypeGPU, not 7.5 (derived; §2.1). And **P = 0.12 is CA's probability for the P90 vector (5.05), not for surpassing (4.85)**: surpassing happens one step earlier, so P(surpass) is strictly greater than 0.12 and **no estimate of it exists in this corpus** — do not read 0.12 as the odds of crossing.

**To aim above that tier.** PixiJS is 5.45 / 62.6 and three.js 6.85 / 78.7. From P90 (5.05) the PixiJS-class gap is **+0.40**, and it decomposes into **six** cells, not two: C2 +0.10, C4 +0.30, C5 +0.10, C7 +0.20, C9 **−0.10**, C10 **−0.20** [re-run]. The +0.40 therefore only holds if TypeShade concedes nothing on agent fit and evidence — the two cells where its P90 already leads — while simultaneously gaining on pain and timing. The tier-jump base rate says why that is hard and where the one opening is: **0 of 6 measured WebGL/GPU-graphics libraries exceeded ×13 deflated growth over 2020–2026** (three.js ×12.5, PixiJS ×6.7, regl ×2.3, glslify ×1.9), while every ≥100× event in the sample happened in the general build/validation layer (esbuild ×1,302, zod ×6,376, astro ×34,043) [measured 2026-09-07, lodash-deflated]. **That base rate excludes TypeGPU by construction and §5.1 now says so** — the index needs a same-week 2020 measurement and TypeGPU was created 2024-02-17, so the rate is silent on post-2020 entrants, and TypeGPU's own ~×160 deflated in twelve months is a live counter-example inside the layer. Two mechanisms produced the sampled jumps at bus factor 1: **transitive carriage by one fast-growing host** (esbuild, taken by Vite at 2,209 downloads/month) and **publishing the interface others target** (`@standard-schema/spec`, 60 lines of types, ×6.34M in 23 months). Both are host-side decisions; neither is buyable with engineering. **And the target moves:** TypeGPU's C1 was priced at ~20k reach; at 60k its composite is 5.00 / 57.5 and with a maturing agent channel 5.10 / 58.6, i.e. **above TypeShade's P90 of 58.0** [re-run].

**Ceiling analysis, stated plainly.** PixiJS-class is **arithmetically within reach** — but the +0.40 is a _net_ of four gains against two concessions TypeShade must refuse to make, so it is not "two cells". **The single most probable route, and its signature metrics.** Not education, not community, not funding. It is **mechanism #1 hedged by #2**: land one host at EE rung 5 while simultaneously publishing the `reflect()` contract as a tiny types-only package that `wgsl_reflect` (277,311/wk), naga wrappers, TypeGPU and `vgpu` (49,779/wk) could each implement, with TypeShade shipping the reference implementation and the conformance matrix. It costs **5 eng-days (§4's Q1 2027 row), not the 8–12 weeks ingestion costs**; it attacks C4 — the only unanimous cell with no code fix — directly; and if a host later replaces the implementation, the interface it targets survives the swap, which is precisely what esbuild did not have. **Two cautions to price honestly:** `@standard-schema/spec` also _un-moated_ zod, making valibot (16.8M/wk) and arktype (1.5M/wk) drop-in substitutes — publishing a contract raises C4 and C7, it does **not** raise C3. And its most likely first implementer, `wgsl_reflect`, is the same substitute §2 names as already ahead of TypeShade on C1 and C7; co-opting it is the point, but it is co-option, not victory.

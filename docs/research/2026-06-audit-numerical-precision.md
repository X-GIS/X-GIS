# Audit ⑥ — Numerical precision & coordinate systems

*Deep-research synthesis, 2026-06-08. File:line audit of the X-GIS precision pipeline merged with GPU large-world precision research (Cesium/Godot/DSFUN) and projection edge-case research (d3-geo/MapLibre/PROJ). Part of the 10-audit series. Claims cited inline.*

---

## TL;DR
The precision architecture is **largely robust and unusually well-tested** — RTC (relative-to-center) + DSFUN double-single hi/lo decomposition keeps Mercator sub-µm at z22 (fuzz-tested), and CPU↔GPU projection forward is parity-checked to ≤1 mm. Two things to correct from the prior shader audit's worry list: (1) the **"DSFUN naming drift"** between shaders (`point` uses `cam_ecef_h/l`, `polygon`/`line` use `cam_ecef_off_h/l`) is **cosmetic — the math is verified identical**, not a divergence; the real gap is the *absence of a cross-shader parity test* tying them. (2) The **deep-zoom f32 drift in non-Mercator projections** (equirect/Natural-Earth, ~0.035–0.11 px at z14-16) is **fundamental float32 physics, not a fixable bug** — and X-GIS is in good company: **MapLibre hits the identical wall and switches globe→Mercator at ~z12** because f32 yields only ~2.5 m at globe scale. The right response is architectural acceptance, not chasing f64 emulation.

---

## A. Precision architecture (as audited)
- **RTC:** tile coordinates are made camera-relative to shrink the magnitude the GPU sees (`view-matrix.ts`, `camera.ts`). This is the standard fix: float32 has 24 effective significand bits (~7 decimal digits) and its ULP **scales with magnitude**, so vertices far from origin jitter — ECEF surface coords (~6.4e6 m) leave only ~1 m of f32 resolution [Cesium "Precisions, Precisions"; demofox ULP].
- **DSFUN:** `shared/src/ecef.ts:143-156` `dsfunSplitECEF()` splits the f64 relative-ECEF into `hi=Math.fround(rel)`, `lo=rel-hi`; the GPU reconstructs `hi+lo` (Kahan-style). Consumed by `polygon.ts:81-82`, `line.ts:92-93` (`cam_ecef_off_h/l`) and `point.ts:53-54` (`cam_ecef_h/l`). Float-float recovers ~44 effective mantissa bits — far better than f32, short of f64 [NVIDIA DSFUN; Godot]. **The non-negotiable invariant** — which X-GIS gets right — is that the precision-preserving subtraction happens in **f64 on the CPU *before* truncation to f32**; doing it in f32 is too late, the low bits are already gone [Cesium; Godot].

## B. Findings (file:line, severity)

1. **DSFUN cross-shader naming drift — LOW (cosmetic), but untested — MEDIUM-as-gap.** `point.ts:53-54` drops the `_off` suffix vs `polygon`/`line`; the audit verified the **semantics are identical** (same subtract-camera-anchor math), so this is documentation debt, not a divergence. The genuine risk: there is **no parity test cross-checking the point vs polygon DSFUN reconstruction** — each path is independently fuzz-tested but never compared, so a future edit to one could silently diverge. **Fix (cheap, high-value):** unify the naming and add a cross-shader DSFUN parity test. [audit RISK1/RISK3]
2. **Deep-zoom f32 drift in non-Mercator — HIGH severity, but FUNDAMENTAL not fixable cheaply.** `matrix.manifest.ts:279-280` (equirect z14) and `:364-365` (NE z14, φ⁶ polynomial + f32 RTC mantissa loss) track ~0.035–0.11 px drift as `expected_red` soft tripwires. Root cause is f32 physics at large coordinate magnitude, compounded by Natural-Earth's φ⁶ polynomial. **This is the same limit MapLibre documents** — globe-mode f32 gives "~1 float32 value per 2.5 m" against Earth's circumference, so MapLibre auto-switches globe→Mercator around z12 [MapLibre globe.md, high]. **Implication:** the realistic fix is *architectural* (use Mercator/tile-local coords at deep zoom, as MapLibre does) rather than f64-emulation in WGSL (expensive) or reformulating projections. The `expected_red` tracking is the correct interim posture. **Test gap:** the drift oracles are GPU-only — no CPU oracle confirms the drift exists equally on both sides (so we can't distinguish f32-loss from a pipeline bug).
3. **Antimeridian seams (equirect/NE) — MEDIUM, projection-interaction not precision.** `matrix.manifest.ts:296-297,381-382`: cross-copy water polygons show T-junction void strips at ±180°. The world-copy offset cancellation is exact in Mercator but the non-linear NE projection distorts the seam. The standard cure is **cut geometry at the antimeridian *before* projection** (RFC 7946 §3.1.9 mandates splitting; d3-geo pre-clips in spherical coords then densifies arcs — naive Cartesian interpolation across [179]→[−179] spans 358° the wrong way) [RFC7946; d3-geo, high]. X-GIS's `project_geom` unwrap handles the primary seam (tested) but not the cross-copy suture. [audit RISK3]
4. **Azi/stereo `flatViewHeightCapM` gap — config, not precision** (`:103-104,509`) — overlaps Audit #2 (desktop). **Oblique polar tearing at 75°N** (`:452-453`) — `MERCATOR_LAT_LIMIT` (85.051129°) interacts with the rotated frame; the Mercator pole singularity is intrinsic (y→∞ at the poles, which is *why* the clamp exists) [Web Mercator, high]. [audit RISK4/RISK5]
5. **Untested GPU inverse round-trip — MEDIUM.** CPU forward/inverse round-trips and CPU↔GPU *forward* parity are tested, but GPU projection *inverse* (if used by label/raster unproject paths) has no parity test, and there are no z14+ round-trip tests. [audit RISK6]

## C. What's robust (positive controls)
Mercator RTC+DSFUN to z22 ≤1 µm GPU-f32 reconstruction (`ecef-precision-fuzz.test.ts`); CPU↔GPU forward parity ≤1 mm at 100 sample points across mercator/equirect/NE (`projection-wgsl-consistency.test.ts`); NE polynomial brought to CPU↔WGSL parity in Phase 2-A (the prior ~8145 km divergence resolved); antimeridian `unwrapLonNear` + NE lobe-wrap tested; the f64-before-truncate invariant correctly implemented in `dsfunSplitECEF`. The documented precision budget (z0 ~1 m → z14 ~1.5 mm → z22 ~1 µm, max zoom 22, `MERCATOR_LAT_LIMIT=85.051129°`) is coherent and matches the float32 ULP math.

## D. Top fixes (ranked)
1. **Accept + document the deep-zoom non-Mercator f32 limit as fundamental, with the MapLibre precedent** (B2) — stop treating it as a bug to chase; if deep-zoom non-Mercator matters, adopt MapLibre's tile-local/Mercator-at-depth strategy. Add a CPU oracle so f32-loss is *distinguishable* from a pipeline regression.
2. **Unify DSFUN naming + add a cross-shader parity test** (B1) — cheap; closes the one genuine (if latent) risk in the "naming drift."
3. **Antimeridian cut-before-project for non-Mercator** (B3) — the d3-geo/RFC7946 approach; or keep the `expected_red` tracking if deep dateline fidelity is out of scope.
